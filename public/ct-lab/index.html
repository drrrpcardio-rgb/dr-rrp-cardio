Calcium score (Agatston)

Non-contrast, 120 kV, prospective ECG-gated, 3 mm slices. Pixels of 130 HU or more are shown in pink. Pick an artery, then click each coronary calcium on the images to assign it. Leave bone and valve calcium alone.

How the Agatston score works
  • Each lesion of at least 1 mm² and 130 HU or more on a 3 mm slice is scored.
  • Score = area (mm²) × density factor from the peak HU: 130–199 → 1, 200–299 → 2, 300–399 → 3, 400+ → 4.
  • Sum all lesions on all slices. CAC-DRS: 0 = none, 1 = 1–99, 2 = 100–299, 3 = 300+.
  • A zero score lowers risk but does not exclude non-calcified plaque in a symptomatic patient.

How to read
  • Pick a vessel. The straightened CPR shows the whole artery; click it to move the cursor and see the cross-section.
  • Turn the CPR with the rotation slider: an eccentric plaque can look mild in one plane and tight in another.
  • The graph under the CPR shows lumen diameter against the expected (reference) diameter.
  • Click or drag on the axial, MPR or 3D images to move the crosshair. Wheel scrolls the slice. Right-drag or Shift-drag sets the window.
  • Use the calcium and stent window to reduce blooming. For stents, add the sharp kernel.
  • Iterative reconstruction lowers noise but makes the texture smoother. The sharp kernel sharpens edges and raises noise.
  • Learn the artefacts: streaks from dense contrast in the SVC and dark beam-hardening bands between dense structures can mimic plaque or perfusion defects.

Structured report

Grade the worst stenosis in each artery (branches count with their parent), then give the CAD-RADS 2.0 category and modifiers.

CAD-RADS 2.0 at a glance
  • 0 0% — no plaque or stenosis
  • 1 1–24% — minimal
  • 2 25–49% — mild
  • 3 50–69% — moderate
  • 4A 70–99% in one or two vessels
  • 4B left main ≥50% or three-vessel ≥70%
  • 5 100% total occlusion
  • N non-diagnostic study
  • Plaque burden P1–P4 (from CAC: 1–100, 101–300, 301–999, >999). Modifiers: HRP high-risk plaque, S stent, G graft, E exceptions (anomaly, dissection, aneurysm), I ischaemia.
